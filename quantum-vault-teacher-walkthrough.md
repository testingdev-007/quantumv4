# The Vault — teacher walkthrough + cold open (6 clips, Flow-ready, 10s)

Ten reference images to generate first, then six fully self-contained 10-second video
prompts — each one complete on its own, nothing deferred to a shared block. Paste any
single prompt into Flow without needing anything else on this page.

## REFERENCE IMAGES — generate these first

Each of these is its own complete image prompt. Generate all ten before touching video
credits; every one you nail here is a re-roll you don't pay for later.

### R1 — The Teacher (character reference)

A confident woman in her late thirties, standing in a three-quarter turn against a
plain seamless dark charcoal backdrop for a clean character reference. She wears a
fitted dark charcoal blazer, sleeves pushed up slightly, over a simple black top — no
jewelry except a plain steel watch. Her hair is pulled back, practical rather than
styled for glamour. Her expression is alert and warm, a faint knowing smile, eyes
bright — the look of someone about to tell you something genuinely great, not a
salesperson's grin. Skin texture is natural: visible pores, fine lines at the eyes, no
airbrushing or beauty smoothing. Lit with a large soft diffused key light from
camera-left at a 45-degree angle, and a subtle cool cyan rim light from behind on her
camera-right shoulder separating her from the black backdrop. Shot on an 85mm portrait
lens at a wide aperture, shallow depth of field, backdrop falling into pure soft black
with no visible seam or floor line. Generate both a head-and-shoulders close crop and a
full-length standing version for continuity across shots.

### R2 — The Movie-Set Environment (wide establishing)

A cavernous dark soundstage at night, black drapes and grip flags swallowing the edges
of frame into total darkness. In the centre of the stage, a partial vault-antechamber
set has been built: a section of polished dark concrete flooring inset into the
soundstage floor, a run of brushed-steel wall panelling, and one heavy circular vault
door standing as a free-standing set piece, visibly braced from behind by scaffolding
poles just barely readable in the gloom. Overhead, a lighting truss holds a row of
hanging Fresnel fixtures and LED panels, throwing warm gold pools of practical light
onto the set's centre — the light falls off sharply at the set's edges into cool
blue-black shadow. Around the built set's perimeter, softly out of focus: two C-stands
with sandbagged bases, a road case with its lid open, a monitor cart whose screen glows
a soft unreadable blue, and coiled cable runs gaffer-taped flat to the floor in neat
lines. Embedded in the set's steel panelling, small cool-cyan practical lights glow
faintly, providing the contrasting accent to the warm gold key. Shot on a 24mm wide
lens, moderately deep focus across the built set itself with the soundstage's outer
edges softening into blur, fine haze in the air catching the truss lighting as visible
beams.

### R3 — The Outer Door (cipher dial, close detail)

A close, richly detailed study of a heavy circular vault door set piece, built from
dressed MDF and sheet steel cladding to read as genuinely load-bearing: matte
dark grey-green paint, real wear and scuffing concentrated around the handle and hinge
points, a few scratches down to bare metal. Set into the door's centre is a large
brass-toned rotating cipher dial: concentric rings of embossed letterforms, deliberately
soft-focus and abstract rather than legible, with a small brass indicator needle fixed
at twelve o'clock. A hidden practical light beneath the dial throws warm gold uplight
across the brass rings, catching their machined edges in bright highlight and casting
long soft shadows up across the door's ridged surface. The background behind the door
falls into soft dark bokeh, with a thin bleed of cool cyan light entering from
camera-left. Shot on a 50mm lens at close range, shallow depth of field, fine dust
motes visible drifting through the gold light beam.

### R4 — Room One (the four-box wall)

A section of brushed-steel shelving bracketed to a concrete-textured set wall, holding
exactly four matte-black cube boxes in a single row at chest height, each roughly
thirty centimetres cubed with sharp, precisely machined edges. Each box has a hairline
seam of cyan light running around its front face, currently dim and unlit. The concrete
wall behind carries visible form-tie holes and faint water-staining streaks for
realism, not a clean poured surface. A single warm gold spotlight hangs directly above
the shelf, pooling light onto the boxes and falling off sharply into cool blue-black
shadow both above and below the shelf line. In the deep background, softly out of
focus, the edge of a C-stand and a loosely coiled cable are just visible, confirming
this is a built set. Shot on a 35mm lens at the boxes' own eye level, shallow depth of
field isolating the shelf sharply from its surroundings.

### R5 — Rooms Two and Three (the scaled box wall)

A vastly larger version of the Room One shelving system, filling an entire built wall
floor to well above head height: eight rows of eight identical matte-black cube boxes
(same scale, same hairline cyan seams, all dim and unlit), the grid receding into soft
darkness at its outer edges where the physical build gives way to raw soundstage black.
Warm gold light spills across the lower-centre portion of the grid from an unseen
source below frame, fading steadily to near-black toward the grid's corners and top. A
tall rolling ladder stands in silhouette at camera-left, and a second lighting truss
crosses high overhead, both confirming the genuine physical scale of the build. Shot on
a 24mm wide lens, camera positioned low and angled slightly upward to exaggerate the
wall's height, focus deep enough that the grid pattern reads clearly across most of the
frame before softening at the far corners.

### R6 — The Ledger (the glowing book set piece)

An oversized prop ledger book bound in worn dark brown leather with tarnished brass
corner reinforcements, lying open on an angled lectern built from dark stained oak with
visible grain and a few authentic scuffs along its edge. The book's open pages are not
paper but a rear-lit panel styled as dozens of thin, perfectly even horizontal glowing
gold light-lines standing in for rows of ledger entries — pure abstract light, no
legible text or characters anywhere. Beside the lectern, a single practical desk lamp
with a green glass banker's shade adds warm incidental pooled light to the surrounding
air, which carries a thin haze thick enough to catch visible volumetric light shafts.
The background falls into soft darkness, with a faint bleed of the soundstage's cool
cyan practicals entering from frame-right. Shot on a 50mm lens from a three-quarter
high angle looking down onto the glowing pages, shallow depth of field with the
lectern's far edge softening out of focus.

### R7 — Vale (character reference, silhouette-forward)

A lean figure in a long dark wool overcoat, collar turned up, wearing plain black
sunglasses despite the low light, deliberately unreadable and composed. The figure is
lit almost entirely in silhouette and rim light: a single hard cyan-white source
positioned behind and to camera-left throws a crisp rim of light along the shoulder,
collar and jawline, while the face itself remains mostly in shadow, with only a faint
reflective glint catching the lenses of the sunglasses. No detail is visible below the
collarbone. The background is total, deep black. Shot on an 85mm lens, tight framing,
shallow depth of field, a thin haze in the air making the rim light visible as a
distinct, crisp-edged shaft rather than a soft glow.

### R8 — The Real Vault Floor (for the cold open — full realism, no set)

A genuinely unglamorous concrete vault antechamber, built and dressed to read as a real
location rather than a set: rough poured concrete flooring with authentic cracks, fine
dust, and scattered small debris; a low chain-link and steel-mesh service corridor
receding into total, undressed darkness at the back of frame; one flickering unshielded
fluorescent tube overhead casting a cold, slightly green-tinted white wash across the
nearest surfaces; and a single harder shaft of blue-white light cutting in at a steep
angle from a door left ajar somewhere off-frame. Crucially, nothing in this image should
read as a film set — no rigging, no stands, no cable runs, no monitors, no visible
scaffolding of any kind. Every surface should look load-bearing, slightly neglected,
and industrial. Shot on a 28mm lens, low angle, framed with a slightly uneven horizon
as if handheld, heavy fine film grain, genuine underexposed shadow detail that doesn't
fully resolve even in the brightest areas of frame.

### R9 — Clapperboard Prop (detail)

A standard black-and-white striped film clapperboard, genuinely well-worn: scuffed
wood grain visible through the paint at the corners, faint chalk dust residue ground
into the slate from countless previous uses. It's held slightly off-centre in frame by
an out-of-focus hand and wrist entering from the bottom edge. The chalked slate area
itself is wiped completely clean and blank — no legible writing, numbers or marks of
any kind. A single hard practical light from above and slightly in front of camera
throws a crisp, well-defined shadow of the clapperboard's hinged sticks across the
slate's surface. The background falls away into soft, featureless black. Shot on a
50mm lens at close range, shallow depth of field with the holding hand rendered soft
and secondary.

### R10 — Phone Prop (screen-glow detail)

An ordinary matte-black smartphone lying face-up on rough, dusty concrete, its screen
lit with a cold, slightly desaturated blue-white glow standing in for an incoming call
screen — no legible app icons, text, or interface elements, just a soft, abstract
glowing rectangle. The glow throws sharp, directional light upward and outward onto the
surrounding dust and loose grit on the concrete immediately around the phone, while
everything beyond a tight radius falls into near-total black. Shot on a 35mm lens at
close, almost macro range, extreme shallow depth of field with the phone's nearest
edge just slightly soft, emphasising the glowing screen as the sharpest point in frame.

## VIDEO CLIP 1 — Cold open on the host: "Tonight, you rob a vault"

**Duration:** 10 seconds. **Ingredients (pin exactly these):** R1 (the teacher), R2
(the movie-set environment), R3 (the outer door, visible in the background).

**Scene** — A cavernous dark soundstage at night. In the centre, a partial
vault-antechamber set: polished dark concrete flooring, brushed-steel wall panelling,
and one heavy circular vault door standing as its own set piece. Overhead, a lighting
truss with hanging Fresnel and LED fixtures throws warm gold pools of light onto the
set's centre, falling off sharply into cool blue-black shadow at the edges. Around the
set's perimeter, softly out of focus: C-stands on sandbagged bases, a road case, a
monitor cart glowing a faint unreadable blue, cable runs taped flat to the floor. Small
cool-cyan practicals are embedded in the steel panelling, contrasting the warm gold key
light. The teacher — confident woman, late thirties, dark charcoal blazer over a black
top, hair pulled back, alert and warm expression — stands beside the vault door at the
start of the shot, small in a wide frame.

**Camera** — 35mm lens, shallow depth of field so the soundstage's rigging stays soft
and secondary throughout. A single continuous slow dolly push, dead straight down the
centre line toward her, starting wide and ending medium-close. No cuts, no handheld,
no zoom — the push is entirely on the dolly track.

**Action, beat by beat**
- 0.0–2.0s — She stands still, arms loose at her sides, looking straight down the lens
  as the dolly begins its slow push in.
- 2.0–5.5s — She starts walking toward camera at the same speed as the dolly track, so
  she holds roughly the same size in frame — a classic matched walk-and-talk.
- 5.5–8.0s — She comes to a stop. Behind her, the vault door set ignites with warm gold
  practical light, one fixture lighting after another in a quick, deliberate sequence.
- 8.0–10.0s — She glances back over her shoulder at the now-lit door, then returns her
  gaze to camera with a small, knowing smile, and holds there as the clip ends.

**Dialogue (she speaks continuously from 0.6s to 9.0s, matched to her walk and stop)**
— *"Tonight, you break into a vault. Six minutes on the clock. And by the time you're
out, you'll have written real code."*

**Sound design** — A low, constant ambient soundstage hum underlies the entire clip.
Her footsteps land audibly and unhurried on the concrete as she walks. Each gold
fixture ignites with a soft, distinct electrical thunk at 5.6s, 6.2s, and 6.8s. Under
the final two seconds, the ambient hum swells very slightly as the gold light behind
her brightens.

**Negative prompt** — No text, captions, subtitles, numbers, logos or legible writing
anywhere in frame. No on-screen UI. No lens flare. No camera shake or handheld wobble.
No zoom. No split screen.

**Avoid** — Her energy should read as "this is obviously about to be great," not a hard
sell — no raised voice, no big arm gestures. The rigging visible at the frame's edges
must stay genuinely out of focus, never sharp enough to distract from her.

## VIDEO CLIP 2 — The Outer Door: the shift cipher

**Duration:** 10 seconds. **Ingredients (pin exactly these):** R1 (the teacher), R3
(the outer door, cipher dial), R2 (the movie-set environment, background only).

**Scene** — The same built vault-door set piece from Clip 1: dressed MDF and steel
cladding painted matte dark grey-green with real wear at the handle and hinges, warm
gold uplight catching the door's large brass-toned cipher dial from a hidden practical
beneath it. The dial's concentric rings of embossed letterforms stay soft-focus and
abstract, never legible, with a small brass indicator needle at twelve o'clock. Behind
the door, the soundstage's darkness and soft out-of-focus rigging are visible but
secondary. The teacher stands at the door in her dark charcoal blazer, one hand resting
near the dial.

**Camera** — 35mm lens, shallow depth of field, the cipher dial in the near-foreground
between her and camera for the opening beat. A slow, continuous lateral dolly move,
tracking left to right as she moves along the length of the door, ending on a static
hold framed dead-on to her face.

**Action, beat by beat**
- 0.0–2.0s — She rests a hand lightly on the dial without turning it yet, looking
  directly at camera.
- 2.0–4.5s — She turns the dial herself. It spins, its letterforms blurring into an
  abstract streak, then slows and settles visibly misaligned. She gives a small,
  unbothered shrug at the miss.
- 4.5–7.0s — She steps sideways along the door as the dolly tracks with her at matching
  speed, gesturing back toward the dial without looking at it herself.
- 7.0–10.0s — She comes to a stop, turns to face camera dead-on, and holds. Behind her,
  the dial begins spinning on its own, faster and faster, blurring into a solid ring of
  gold light by the end of the shot.

**Dialogue**
- 2.1–4.3s — *"The combination's hidden behind a cipher. Guess wrong, and the clock
  doesn't care."*
- 7.2–10.0s — *"So you won't guess. You'll write something that keeps trying until
  it's right."*

**Sound design** — A metallic scraping spin as the dial turns at 2.1s. A soft, dull
failed-click as it settles misaligned at 4.3s. Her footsteps are audible but quiet
during the dolly track. A low ambient tension bed rises gently under the second line of
dialogue and continues under the self-spinning dial in the final beat, which carries
its own rising mechanical whirr.

**Negative prompt** — No text, captions, subtitles, numbers, logos or legible writing
anywhere in frame, and the dial's letterforms must remain motion-blurred or abstract at
all times, never resolving into readable characters. No on-screen UI. No lens flare. No
camera shake. No zoom. No split screen.

**Avoid** — The misaligned stop at 4.5s must look like a genuine, plausible attempt,
not a random flail. The self-spinning dial in the final beat should feel eerie and
deliberate, not comedic.

## VIDEO CLIP 3 — Room One: four boxes, superposition

**Duration:** 10 seconds. **Ingredients (pin exactly these):** R1 (the teacher), R4
(Room One box wall), R2 (the movie-set environment, background only).

**Scene** — A section of brushed-steel shelving on a concrete-textured set wall,
holding four matte-black cube boxes in a single row at chest height, each with a
hairline cyan seam around its front face, currently dim. The concrete wall carries
visible form-tie holes and faint water-staining. A single warm gold spotlight hangs
directly above the shelf, pooling onto the boxes and falling into cool blue-black
shadow above and below. Softly out of focus in the deep background, the edge of a
C-stand and a loosely coiled cable confirm this is a built set. The teacher approaches
from frame-left.

**Camera** — 35mm lens, shallow depth of field. A slow, continuous dolly-in as she
walks toward the shelf, settling into a static medium-wide hold for the final beats
once she steps back from the boxes.

**Action, beat by beat**
- 0.0–2.5s — She walks in from frame-left toward the box shelf, already talking as she
  moves, dolly tracking in with her.
- 2.5–4.5s — She reaches the first box and opens it by hand: empty inside, its cyan
  seam staying dark. She closes it and is already moving toward the second box without
  breaking stride, opening and closing it the same way.
- 4.5–6.0s — She stops beside the shelf, hand hovering just above the third box's lid,
  deliberately not opening it, looking at camera.
- 6.0–10.0s — She steps back from the shelf. All four boxes' seams ignite into
  shimmering cyan light simultaneously, in perfect unison, and hold there, pulsing
  gently in place for the remainder of the shot.

**Dialogue**
- 0.2–4.3s — *"Four boxes, one key. Open them one at a time, and sometimes you get
  unlucky."*
- 6.2–10.0s — *"Or don't choose at all. Let every box hold the answer at once."*

**Sound design** — Her footsteps as she walks in. A soft hinge-click each time she
opens and closes the first two boxes (at roughly 2.7s and 3.6s). A brief beat of near
silence as she hesitates over the third box. A rising electronic shimmer builds from
6.0s as the four seams ignite, settling into a steady, even pulsing tone that holds to
the end of the clip.

**Negative prompt** — No text, captions, subtitles, numbers, logos or legible writing
anywhere in frame, including on the boxes themselves. No on-screen UI. No lens flare.
No camera shake. No zoom. No split screen.

**Avoid** — The four-box ignition at 6.0s must be perfectly simultaneous and even
across all four — no sequential lighting, no single box lagging behind the others.

## VIDEO CLIP 4 — Rooms Two and Three: the wall multiplies

**Duration:** 10 seconds. **Ingredients (pin exactly these):** R1 (the teacher), R5

**Camera** — Opens on a 35mm lens close-ish on the teacher in front of a small,

rises, ending on a 24mm-equivalent wide shot with the teacher small in frame.

**Action, beat by beat**
- 0.0–2.0s — Close-ish on her standing in front of a small, four-box section of wall,

- 2.0–5.5s — The camera begins its slow crane pull-back and rise. Additional shelving

anywhere in frame, including any counter or digital readout. No on-screen UI. No lens
flare. No camera shake beyond the intended smooth crane movement. No handheld wobble.

**Avoid** — The shelving must read as heavy and genuinely mechanical when it slides in

## VIDEO CLIP 5 — The Ledger: the oracle, and the clapperboard handoff

**Duration:** 10 seconds. **Ingredients (pin exactly these):** R1 (the teacher), R6
(the ledger set), R9 (the clapperboard prop).

**Scene** — An oversized prop ledger bound in worn dark brown leather with tarnished
brass corners, open on an angled dark oak lectern. Its open pages are rendered as
dozens of thin, even horizontal glowing gold light-lines standing in for rows of
entries — pure abstract light, nothing legible. A green-shaded desk lamp beside the
lectern adds warm pooled light to hazy air thick enough to show volumetric beams. The
teacher stands beside the lectern, one hand resting near the glowing pages.

**Camera** — First half: a static medium-close shot on a 50mm lens, teacher and ledger
both in frame, shallow depth of field. At the midpoint, a hard cut to a locked-off
close shot of the clapperboard prop, held by an out-of-focus hand entering from the
bottom of frame, for the second half.

**Action, beat by beat**
- 0.0–2.5s — She runs a finger slowly down the glowing gold rows, unhurried, talking as
  she does.
- 2.5–4.5s — Hard cut within the same static framing: the entire ledger flares cyan,
  every row lit at once, and then one row near the centre snaps instantly to red and
  holds, every other row going dark in the same instant.
- 4.5–5.5s — She looks up from the red line directly to camera, an expression of
  genuine, unstaged delight.
- 5.5–6.0s — Hard cut: the clapperboard fills the frame, held slightly off-centre, its
  chalked slate wiped clean and blank.
- 6.0–10.0s — The clapperboard's sticks clap shut, hard and decisive, and the shot
  holds on the closed board for the remainder of the clip.

**Dialogue**
- 0.2–3.8s — *"Sixty-four accounts. One rule. Read them by hand, and the clock
  bleeds."*
- 4.0–5.4s — *"Hand the same rule to a quantum machine — it just answers."*
- No line plays over the clapperboard beat — the clap itself is the intended button on
  the clip.

**Sound design** — Soft paper-static ambience underlies the first line. A low
electronic swell rises as the ledger turns cyan at 2.5s. One sharp, resonant tone
sounds as the red line snaps into place. Ambient sound drops to near-total silence for
a half-second just before the clapperboard beat, then the clap itself lands as one
loud, dry, percussive sound — the single loudest, driest sound in the entire clip —
followed by near silence holding to the end.

**Negative prompt** — No text, captions, subtitles, numbers, logos or legible writing
anywhere in frame, including on the ledger's rows and the clapperboard's slate, which
must remain completely blank. No on-screen UI. No lens flare. No camera shake. No zoom
during either half. No split screen.

**Avoid** — The red flare at 4.5s must be an instant, hard snap — never a fade or
dissolve. The clapperboard's slate stays entirely blank throughout; nothing chalked or
written on it at any point.

## VIDEO CLIP 6 — The real thing: cold open, first-person, no set visible

**Duration:** 10 seconds. **Ingredients (pin exactly these):** R8 (the real vault
floor, full realism), R10 (the phone prop), R7 (Vale, silhouette — implied via voice
only in this clip, not seen).

**Scene** — A genuinely unglamorous concrete vault antechamber: rough poured concrete
flooring with real cracks and dust, a low chain-link and steel-mesh service corridor
receding into total undressed darkness, one flickering unshielded fluorescent tube
overhead casting a cold, faintly green-white wash, and a single harder shaft of
blue-white light entering at a steep angle from a door left ajar somewhere off-frame.
Nothing in this location should read as a film set — no rigging, no stands, no visible
production equipment of any kind. This is the one clip in the set that must feel
completely, unbrokenly real.

**Camera** — Strict first-person point-of-view throughout — the audience is the
protagonist's own eyes, and no other character or reflection of the protagonist is ever
seen. No dolly, no crane, no stabiliser: a slow, slightly unsteady handheld drift
consistent with someone just regaining consciousness, the one clip in this set where
visible camera imperfection is intentional and correct rather than a flaw. Lens
equivalent to 28mm for an appropriately wide, disorienting field of view.

**Action, beat by beat**
- 0.0–2.0s — Total black screen. A low, muffled electronic ringtone begins, as if heard
  through a fogged, half-conscious head.
- 2.0–3.5s — Vision swims gradually into focus: an ordinary black smartphone lying
  face-up on rough concrete inches from camera, its screen lit with a cold blue-white
  glow, pulsing gently with each ring.
- 3.5–6.0s — The POV lifts and turns, unsteady, taking in the cavernous dark concrete
  antechamber for the first time: the flickering fluorescent tube, the mesh corridor
  vanishing into darkness, and the single harder shaft of blue-white light cutting
  across the space from an unseen door.
- 6.0–8.0s — A gloved hand — the protagonist's own, seen only in first-person — reaches
  down into frame and picks up the still-buzzing phone, turning it to answer the call.
- 8.0–10.0s — The call connects. Through the phone's speaker, a voice — Vale's, sharp
  and fast, never seen on screen in this clip — speaks the final line as a low, distant
  klaxon begins rising underneath, and somewhere deep in the background a red emergency
  light starts to strobe.

**Dialogue (Vale, heard only through the phone speaker, 8.2–10.0s)** — *"You're awake.
Good. The alarm's tripped — you've got six minutes."*

**Sound design** — A muffled, distant ringtone plays from 0.0s, sharpening in clarity
as focus returns around 2.0–3.5s. A low, distant klaxon begins almost imperceptibly
around 4.5s and rises steadily and continuously to the end of the clip. The scrape of
the gloved hand against concrete is audible as it reaches for the phone at 6.0s. A
sharp, distinct click sounds as the call connects at 8.0s. Vale's voice is
deliberately compressed and tinny, as if genuinely passing through a real phone
speaker, not clean studio dialogue with a filter layered on top.

**Negative prompt** — No visible protagonist body, face, or reflection at any point —
first-person hands only. No legible text, icons, or interface elements on the phone
screen. No visible set edges, rigging, stands, cables, or any other production
equipment of any kind — this clip alone must read as completely, unbrokenly real. No
captions or subtitles. No on-screen UI. No lens flare. No zoom. No split screen.

**Avoid** — Vale's dialogue must sound genuinely phone-compressed and slightly
degraded, not like clean dialogue with an obvious filter slapped over it. The handheld
drift should feel like disorientation, not a shaky-cam gimmick — keep the movement slow
and physically plausible for someone just waking up.
